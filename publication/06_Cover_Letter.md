# Cover letter

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider my manuscript, “Scale Trade-offs in Within-Sample Self-Normalization for Cross-Study Generalization in Public cfDNA Cohorts”, for Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine.

This study distinguishes three properties that are often combined in public cfDNA evaluation: the genomic reference scale of normalization, dependence on companion patients, and transfer of a source-locked operating threshold. The primary evidence is a bidirectional study-held-out comparison of 548 participants; all analyses use an existing 585-participant public cohort.

Paired Hybrid−Global AUROC differences were -0.024 in Cristiano and +0.018 in Jiang. The manuscript therefore presents a target-dependent scale trade-off. A mathematical invariance argument, repeated real-patient anchors, source-study-held-out calibration and chromosome-block inference establish distinct tests of preprocessing and operating-point transport. At fixed case fraction 0.50, the evaluated EB-TargetMap rule changed anchor decisions in 0.140 and 0.126 of repeated evaluations, whereas the sample-wise rules satisfy companion independence by construction.

The accompanying reproducible code includes automated leakage and invariance tests. No new human recruitment or specimen collection was performed. Study source is treated as a proxy for technical and population differences.

The work is original and is not under consideration at another journal. A previous version was submitted to bioRxiv as BIORXIV/2026/761726 Version 1 and is awaiting screening; this local revision has not been uploaded.
